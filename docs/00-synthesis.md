# Synthesis: Report of the Cross-Disciplinary Committee

**Subject of review:** 「养猫是世界上绝大多数男性一生中唯一能够合法购买的性服务」 — "Keeping a cat is the only 'sexual service' that the vast majority of men in the world can legally purchase in their lifetime."

**Committee:** nine disciplines, nine papers, one cat. The cat did not attend.

## 1. The Result

The committee's verdict is unanimous, which in scholarship is usually a sign of fraud and in this case is a sign of a well-built joke:

| # | Discipline | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Economics | Holds with redefinition |
| 02 | Law | Holds with redefinition |
| 03 | Sociology | Holds with redefinition |
| 04 | Psychology | Holds with redefinition |
| 05 | Linguistics | Holds with redefinition |
| 06 | History & Anthropology | Holds with redefinition |
| 07 | Philosophy | Holds with redefinition |
| 08 | Public health | Holds with redefinition |
| 09 | Demography | Holds with redefinition |

Zero papers found the thesis true as literally stated. Zero found it simply false. All nine independently performed the same operation: confirm every empirical premise, reject the central label, substitute a redefined label of their own, and declare the thesis true under the substitute. Section 4 addresses what this unanimity actually means.

## 2. Where the Disciplines Agree

**The prohibition premise is solid.** Law (02) surveys the regimes and finds purchase criminalized for the buyer under both full criminalization and the Nordic model. Demography (09) weights that survey by population: China and India alone contribute roughly 1.44 billion men under prohibition; summing only the largest prohibitory states already passes 3.2 billion, about 78 percent of the world's roughly 4.1 billion men, plausibly rising to 85 to 95 percent once smaller prohibitory jurisdictions are counted. The permissive counterweight (Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, parts of Australia, licensed Nevada counties) totals at most a few hundred million men. "The vast majority" survives every threshold Demography tested, including 90 percent.

**The legality premise is solid.** Every discipline that checked found cat acquisition legal essentially everywhere, openly advertised, taxed, insured, and, per Sociology (03), preceded by a home check intended to protect the companion, a feature no other transaction of the parodied type has ever offered.

**The delivered bundle is real.** Economics (01) describes it in characteristics terms: for $50 to $200 upfront plus $300 to $1,000 or more per year, the buyer obtains co-residence, tactile contact, co-sleeping, and persistent demands for affection. Psychology (04) confirms the bundle engages real systems: roughly half or more of owners in surveys sleep with the cat, petting is associated with modest cortisol reduction, and baby schema recruits caregiving. History (06) confirms the bundle is modern — the indoor, provisioned, bed-sharing cat is a 20th-century product of commercial food and litter — and notes the companion solicits affection on her own terms and is forgiven, which it calls the most durable finding in the literature.

**The literal claim is false.** On this the agreement is total and unhedged. Law: a cat is a chattel, not a service provider. Psychology: attachment, caregiving, and mating are dissociable systems. Philosophy (07): if it were truly the named service, the cat's inability to consent would make it a grave wrong rather than a loophole; since it is not, there is no service, only stewardship.

## 3. Where the Disciplines Clash

**The dog problem.** Economics objects against the thesis and, awkwardly, against itself: dogs deliver an overlapping bundle, equally legally, so the cat is not the *only* legal entrant, merely the cheapest and lowest-maintenance one. Law and Linguistics reply that the uniqueness was never economic: under the thesis's own lexical rules the cat is in a category of one, because only the cat carries the name. The clash is genuine and unresolved: uniqueness in the market fails, uniqueness in the lexicon holds.

**Who serves whom.** Philosophy's second thought experiment and Sociology's account of emotional labour converge on an embarrassment none of the other papers fully faces: the observed flow of services runs from the man to the cat (feeding, litter, veterinary bills, a 5 a.m. summons delivered by standing on his chest). Economics concedes the transaction is closer to adoption of a dependent than to purchase of a service. The synthesis records this as the committee's most quietly devastating finding: if any service is being purchased, the evidence suggests the man is the staff.

**Substitution: real or illusory?** Public health (08) makes the boldest positive claim: like a nicotine patch, the cat partially substitutes for a riskier market, delivering companionship with complete elimination of that market's STI and violence risk profile. Psychology (04) supplies the limit: the systems do not substitute for each other, strongly attached owners remain interested in human partnership, and the satisfaction is of a different system than the one the thesis names. The committee resolves this as follows: substitution holds for the companionship component of demand, and only for that component. How large that component is, no paper measured, and the committee notes the gap.

**Scope conditions nobody announced.** History restricts the claim to the modern era; Demography restricts it to residence at a point in time, not lifetime exposure (travel and legal change erode "only in a lifetime"); Linguistics restricts it to English and a few analogues such as French *chatte*. The thesis as stated claims the world and a lifetime. The thesis as defended claims the Anglophone present. The committee regards this shrinkage as the true cost of the nine redefinitions.

## 4. The Definition Problem

The whole claim lives or dies on how 性服务 ("sexual service") is defined, and the nine papers sort neatly into three definitions:

1. **Literal definition** (an act, performed by a person, as such): the thesis fails, 9 to 0. No paper dissented; Linguistics adds that under this definition the sentence is not even false so much as not one proposition, since it survives only by switching the sense of *pussy* mid-utterance, and a pun requires two senses while a proposition permits one.
2. **Functional definition** (purchased, sustained access to co-resident, tactile, affection-demanding companionship from a female-coded companion): the thesis holds, 9 to 0, and holds with margins that surprised the committee: population-weighted prohibition of 85 to 95 percent on one side, near-universal legality and a roughly $150-billion-a-year United States pet industry on the other.
3. **Lexical definition** (a statement about what English permits a speaker to say): the thesis holds trivially, because the cat sense of *pussy* is attested from the 16th century and historically prior to the slang sense; the joke reactivates an old word rather than inventing one. Under this definition the thesis is a true report about a dictionary.

The definition problem is therefore not a flaw in the thesis; it is the thesis. The sentence is a load-bearing pun: remove the equivocation and it reduces, in Linguistics' words, to "the true but trivial claim that men may legally buy cats"; keep it, and nine disciplines' worth of real data (prohibition shares, adoption fees, co-sleeping rates, domestication dates) can be hung on it without any of the data becoming false. The facts are all genuine. Only the hook is a word. The committee also notes the translation asymmetry that explains the thesis's origin: Chinese 性服务 has no feline sense, so the joke had to be built in Chinese syntax on imported English polysemy, and it survives the trip precisely because its author and audience are bilingual. A monolingual world would never have produced it, which Demography is invited to treat as a footnote and Linguistics as the entire paper.

## 5. Mock Peer Review: Final Verdict

*Reviewer 1:* The empirical work is, regrettably, sound. The population weighting in Paper 09 and the comparative survey in Paper 02 establish the premises beyond reasonable dispute. Recommend acceptance of the premises and rejection of the title.

*Reviewer 2:* The manuscript uses one word in two senses and hopes the referees will not notice. This referee noticed. This referee also could not stop noticing that every objection he drafted had already been drafted, more politely, by one of the nine papers. The work is self-refuting in a way that constitutes its contribution.

*Reviewer 3 (the cat):* No comments. Slept through the proceedings on the warmest available surface.

**Editorial decision: Accept, with major redefinition.** The thesis 「养猫是世界上绝大多数男性一生中唯一能够合法购买的性服务」 is classified by this committee as **false as stated, true as redefined unanimously in a single consistent direction, and therefore a sound joke**: empirically armoured, legally literate, demographically weighted, and semantically hinged on one 16th-century English word. The committee's headline conclusion is that the sentence's comedy and its correctness have the same source — the facts are real, and only the label is contraband, which is, in the end, exactly what the sentence said about the cat.

*The committee thanks the author for the submission and reminds him that the companion acquired in the course of this research is not a service, cannot consent to being cited, and is due dinner.*
