# The Denominator Problem: A Demographic Audit of a Feline Claim

## 1. Operational Definitions

The thesis under review is: 「养猫是世界上绝大多数男性一生中唯一能够合法购买的性服务」

In English, the joke turns on "pussy," which denotes both a cat and, vulgarly, something else entirely. A demographic analysis cannot adjudicate a pun, but it can audit the population claim attached to it.

Three terms require operationalization.

First, "the vast majority of men." The reference population is the world's male population, approximately 4.1 billion persons in the mid-2020s. "Vast majority" is not a standard statistical threshold. For sensitivity purposes, we test three definitions: a supermajority above 66 percent, a strong majority above 75 percent, and a near-consensus above 90 percent.

Second, "legally purchase." We code a man by his jurisdiction of usual residence and ask whether an adult may lawfully pay for the literal, non-feline service there. This is a de jure coding, not a measure of enforcement, availability, or behavior.

Third, "only." This is the strongest word in the thesis. For it to hold for an individual, the set of such services he may legally purchase over his entire lifetime must contain exactly one element, supplied here by lawful acquisition of a female cat who thereafter resides with him, seeks physical proximity, and sleeps in shared domestic space. Cat acquisition is coded as a purchase or adoption fee plus continuing expenditure on food, litter, and veterinary care.

## 2. Population Weighting

Unweighted counts of countries are misleading. Roughly 190 jurisdictions exist, but men are not distributed evenly among them. A small permissive state and India should not receive equal weight.

A rough population-weighted calculation proceeds as follows. China contributes approximately 720 million men and prohibits the purchase in question. India contributes approximately 720 million men, likewise under prohibition in practice and law. The United States contributes approximately 170 million men, with purchase illegal in nearly all counties. Indonesia, Pakistan, Brazil, Bangladesh, Russia, Mexico, Japan, and the Philippines together contribute well over another 800 million men, under regimes that are prohibitory, criminalizing, or heavily restrictive of purchase.

Summing only these largest prohibitory populations already exceeds 3.2 billion men, or roughly 78 percent of the global male total:

3.2 billion / 4.1 billion ≈ 0.78

Adding the many smaller prohibitory jurisdictions in Africa, the Middle East, and Asia plausibly raises the share to between 85 and 95 percent. The coding is imprecise at the margins, particularly where sale is tolerated but purchase, solicitation, or third-party involvement is criminalized. The interval, however, is robust: under any plausible coding, the permissive share is a minority.

The permissive counterweight is demographically small. Germany accounts for roughly 42 million men, the Netherlands for about 9 million, Australia for about 13 million, and New Zealand for about 2.5 million. Even adding other legal or decriminalized jurisdictions, several parts of Latin America, and the licensed counties of Nevada, the total remains in the low hundreds of millions at most, plausibly 4 to 10 percent of all men.

On the feline side, supply is not a binding constraint. The global pet cat population is in the hundreds of millions. In the United States alone, roughly 37 million households keep cats. Extrapolated globally, male cat owners number comfortably in the tens of millions, likely in the low hundreds of millions. The transaction is therefore legal, common, and demographically observable.

## 3. The Strongest Case For the Thesis

In its strongest honest form, the thesis is a claim about legal opportunity sets for the median man, not about conduct.

Take a randomly selected man from the global population. With probability of roughly 0.85 to 0.95, he resides in a jurisdiction where purchasing the literal service is illegal. With near certainty, he resides somewhere he may lawfully acquire and keep a cat, barring local tenancy or municipal restrictions. His lawful opportunity set therefore contains the feline option and excludes the literal one.

The claim survives every threshold proposed above. It holds at 66 percent, at 75 percent, and, on the upper estimates, at 90 percent. The conclusion does not depend on finely balanced data. It depends on the brute demographic fact that the world's most populous countries are, on this question, prohibitory. Demography here rewards size, and size is concentrated in prohibition.

## 4. The Strongest Objection

The objection is that "only in a lifetime" converts a cross-sectional residence statistic into a longitudinal exposure claim, and the data do not support that conversion.

Residence is not destiny. Men migrate and travel. A man coded as prohibitory at age 25 may spend a weekend, a posting, or a retirement in a permissive jurisdiction. Lifetime exposure therefore requires panel data on mobility, not a snapshot of current residence. Even a modest annual probability of travel to a permissive jurisdiction compounds over fifty adult years into a substantial cumulative probability.

Second, a minority is not a small number. If permissive jurisdictions contain only 5 percent of the world's men, that is still approximately 205 million people:

0.05 × 4.1 billion ≈ 205 million

A claim can thus be true for the vast majority and false for a population larger than that of most countries.

Third, the law changes within a lifetime. Regimes legalize, criminalize, and decriminalize on timescales shorter than a human lifespan. A cohort coded under prohibition at birth may not die under it.

Finally, uptake exposes a gap between opportunity and incidence. Tens of millions of men own cats, but billions do not. For most men, the feline transaction is legally available and never exercised, which makes "only purchasable option" defensible but "purchased service" empirically rare.

These objections do not overturn the population weighting. They narrow its scope, from a lifetime certainty to a statement about legal residence at a given time.

**Verdict: Holds with redefinition — true as a population-weighted statement about residence and legal opportunity, not as a lifetime guarantee for every man in the majority.**
